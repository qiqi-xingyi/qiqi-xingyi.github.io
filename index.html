|About⟩

I am a PhD student in quantum biological computing, advised by Dr. Qiang Guan at Kent State University and Dr. Feixiong Cheng at Cleveland Clinic. My main research area is quantum computing, including algorithms, architectures, and systems, and I apply it to problems in structural biology and drug discovery.

Most of my projects start from a concrete task, such as predicting the structure of a peptide in a binding pocket, and ask what today's quantum processors can actually contribute to it. My work has run on IBM utility-scale quantum hardware, including the IBM quantum processor at Cleveland Clinic. In 2026 I joined the NSF I-Corps for Quantum+X program as Entrepreneurial Lead for a hybrid quantum-classical drug discovery platform.

|Research Interests⟩

Quantum algorithms for protein structure

I formulate structure prediction for short peptides and binding-site fragments as problems a quantum processor can work on directly. That has meant encoding amino-acid chains on a lattice as Hamiltonians and solving them with VQE, replacing iterative optimization with Hamiltonian sampling, and fusing the resulting quantum energy landscapes with statistical potentials from deep learning. Evaluated against AlphaFold3 and classical baselines, these methods have improved accuracy on the short, flexible fragments where data-driven models have the least to go on.

QSADHybrid Quantum-AIBinding-Site Framework

Quantum systems, benchmarks, and HPC integration

Running these workflows on real hardware raises systems questions, such as how quantum and classical HPC resources should share sampling and post-processing, and how much hardware noise a method can tolerate. I build datasets and tools for this, including an open docking benchmark of structures predicted on quantum computers, and a certifiable compression method that reduces the classical cost of processing quantum-chemistry Hamiltonians.

Quantum Sampling Architecture for Protein Structure Reconstruction on Utility-Scale Hardware

Yuqi Zhang, Bo Fang, Yuxin Yang, Feixiong Cheng, Jieyang Chen, Sherry Fang, Siwei Chen, Junhan Zhao, Qiang Guan*

SC '26To appear

QSAD recasts structure prediction for peptides in binding pockets as Hamiltonian sampling, replacing iterative optimization. Run entirely on IBM Heron R2 over 101 peptides, it was 27–71% more accurate than every AI and quantum baseline we tested and used 27× less quantum time than VQE.

arXivScholar

A Hybrid Quantum-AI Framework for Protein Structure Prediction on NISQ Devices

Yuqi Zhang, Yuxin Yang, Feixiong Cheng, Cheng-Chang Lu, Nima Saeidi, Junhan Zhao, Siwei Chen, Weiwen Jiang, Qiang Guan*

ACM Transactions on Quantum Computing, 2026To appear

We fuse VQE energies from a 127-qubit IBM processor with secondary-structure and dihedral-angle statistics from the NSP3 network. The combined energy picks out native-like structures more reliably, reaching a mean RMSD of 4.9 Å on 75 protein fragments, ahead of AlphaFold3 and ColabFold.

arXivScholarCited by 4

A Quantum Framework for Protein Binding-Site Structure Prediction on Utility-Level Quantum Processors

Yuqi Zhang, Yuxin Yang, William Martin, Kingsten Lin, Zixu Wang, Cheng-Chang Lu, Weiwen Jiang, Ruth Nussinov, Joseph Loscalzo, Qiang Guan*, Feixiong Cheng*

Advanced Science, 2025

We pose structure prediction for short, flexible binding-site fragments as a VQE ground-state problem on a tetrahedral lattice, and ran every experiment on the IBM quantum processor at Cleveland Clinic. On 30 fragments the predictions beat AlphaFold3 and classical baselines in both RMSD and docking.

DOIScholarCited by 17

QDockBank: A Dataset for Ligand Docking on Protein Fragments Predicted on Utility-Level Quantum Computers

Yuqi Zhang, Yuxin Yang, Cheng-Chang Lu, Weiwen Jiang, Feixiong Cheng, Bo Fang, Qiang Guan*

SC '25

A dataset of protein fragment structures predicted on utility-level quantum computers, built for ligand docking. Each fragment comes with its quantum run metadata (qubits, circuit depth, energies, runtime) and its docking and RMSD results.

ACM DLArtifactsScholarCited by 7

Qronecker: A Certifiable Kronecker Compression Primitive for Quantum-Chemistry Hamiltonians

arXiv, 2026Preprint

Qronecker compresses quantum-chemistry qubit Hamiltonians with low-rank Kronecker decompositions computed directly in Pauli coefficient space, and returns a worst-case energy certificate for each choice of rank and cut. Evaluated on hundreds of molecules up to 30 qubits.
