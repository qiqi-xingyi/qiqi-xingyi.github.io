About

I am a PhD student in quantum biological computing at Kent State University and Cleveland Clinic, advised by Dr. Qiang Guan and Dr. Feixiong Cheng. My core research is quantum computing — algorithms, architectures and systems.

Research

Hover a stage to see the related papers

I build the algorithmic and systems stack for quantum biocomputing, treating quantum computing and computational biology as a single cross-layer research program rather than two adjacent fields. Biological questions determine what information a quantum workflow must recover, while real hardware and system constraints shape the representations, algorithms, sampling strategies, and QC–HPC architectures used to recover it. Across these layers, I study quantum architectures, real-hardware execution and benchmarking, protein structure and conformational landscapes, binding-site prediction, molecular docking, and drug discovery. Biology determines what a useful answer must contain; hardware determines what can be computed; cross-layer co-design closes the gap between them.
